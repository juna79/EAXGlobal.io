The problem

Organisations decide on digital evidence they cannot fully trust.

Almost every important decision now depends on a digital file. A bank approves a facility against uploaded statements. An insurer settles a claim against a repair invoice and an assessor's report. A buyer pays a supplier against a delivery note and a certificate.

But files can be altered, duplicated, replaced or generated convincingly. As editing and generation tools become more capable, the gap between a document that looks genuine and one that is genuine keeps widening — and the cost of relying on the wrong version falls on the organisation that trusted it.

The missing layer

Most systems manage information. Few can prove it.

Organisations already run capable systems for storing, moving and processing documents. What almost none of them have is an independent way to establish whether the specific file in front of them is the original, unaltered version issued by a known source.

Trust infrastructure is the layer that answers that question. It records a cryptographic fingerprint of a document at the moment it is issued, then lets anyone check a later copy against that record — without the original file ever needing to be exposed. It does not replace existing systems. It sits alongside them and gives them something to verify against.

How we build

Principles behind the technology.

Privacy by default

Verification works from a cryptographic fingerprint of a document, so the original file does not need to be exposed to be checked.

Independent of any one system

The trust layer sits alongside existing software. It adds a way to verify records without asking organisations to replace what already works.

Verify before you decide

The point of the infrastructure is to establish whether evidence is genuine before capital, claims or approvals move against it.
